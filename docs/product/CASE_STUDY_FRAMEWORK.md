# Enterprise Case Study Framework

**Published case studies: 0.**

Not a placeholder. EcoIQ has not executed a project through to measured
outcome, so there is nothing to publish, and the section does not appear in the
product.

This document defines what one has to contain — so that when the first is
written, the bar is already set and cannot be negotiated downward under
commercial pressure.

---

## Why zero, stated plainly

A case study is the strongest claim a B2B product makes. It says: *this
happened, we caused it, and here is the measurement.*

Writing one from a pilot that was scoped but not executed, or executed but not
measured, is the same category of fabrication as a substituted score — except
that a prospect may commit capital on the strength of it.

Every counter in the product is derived. This one is too: **0**, and the
section is omitted rather than showing it.

---

## Required structure

A study may be published only when **all seven** are present. Six is not a case
study with a gap; it is not a case study.

### 1. Baseline
What was true before, **measured**, with provenance. Not "energy costs were
high" — the figure, its source, and when it was taken.

### 2. Problem
The specific decision that needed making, and what made it hard. If the
problem statement could be reused for another client unchanged, it is
marketing, not a problem statement.

### 3. Evidence
What EcoIQ actually held, at what coverage and confidence. **Including the
gaps.** A study that presents only the evidence that supported the
recommendation is an argument, not a record.

### 4. Recommendation
What EcoIQ proposed, and **what it advised against**. The alternatives
considered and rejected, with the reason.

### 5. Capital
What it cost. Committed, deployed and — where they differ — why.

### 6. Execution
What was actually done, by whom, over what period. Including what changed from
the plan, because something always does, and a study where nothing did is
either trivial or incomplete.

### 7. Measured impact
The outcome, **measured the same way as the baseline**, by a method that would
have detected no change. Attribution stated honestly: what else was happening
that could explain the result.

---

## Verification bar

| requirement | why |
|---|---|
| Baseline and outcome measured by the **same method** | otherwise the delta measures the method change |
| Outcome **independently verifiable** | the client can confirm it, and would |
| Client has **reviewed and approved** the text | including the parts that are unflattering |
| Evidence coverage recorded **at the time**, not reconstructed | a reconstructed figure is an estimate wearing a measurement's clothes |
| Counter-explanations stated | if the plant also cut production 30%, the study says so |

---

## What disqualifies a study

- **Modelled outcomes presented as measured.** A projection is a projection
  however good the model.
- **Selective baselines.** Choosing the worst month to compare against.
- **Attribution by adjacency.** EcoIQ was involved and the number improved,
  therefore EcoIQ improved it.
- **Anonymised to the point of unfalsifiability.** "A major European
  manufacturer" that cannot be identified even under NDA is not evidence.
- **No stated uncertainty.** Every measurement has some.

---

## Until then

What EcoIQ can honestly show today:

- the **method** — how evidence, coverage, confidence and eligibility work
- the **system**, inspectably, on real data
- what it **refuses to assert**, which is itself a strong signal to the kind of
  buyer who has been shown a lot of confident dashboards

The absence of case studies is legible and defensible. A fabricated one would
not survive the first serious diligence conversation — and the product's entire
proposition is that it does not fabricate.
